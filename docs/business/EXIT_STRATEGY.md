# Logivox Exit Strategy Framework

**Prepared:** January 2026  
**Planning Horizon:** 6-36 Months  
**Confidential Strategic Document**

---

## Strategic Overview

This document outlines three distinct exit pathways for Logivox, each with different risk profiles, timelines, and expected outcomes. The optimal path depends on risk tolerance, time availability, and desired return levels.

---

## Exit Option 1: Quick Asset Sale

### Strategy Summary

Immediate sale of platform as complete technology asset to strategic or financial buyer seeking to enter or expand in WMS market.

### Timeline

**Total Duration:** 6-12 months

**Phase Breakdown:**

- Preparation and packaging: 1-2 months
- Buyer identification and outreach: 1-2 months
- Initial discussions and NDAs: 1 month
- Due diligence: 2-3 months
- Negotiation and closing: 1-2 months

### Target Buyers

**Primary Targets:**

- Mid-market WMS companies seeking feature enhancement
- Private equity firms with supply chain portfolio focus
- 3PL operators wanting proprietary technology
- Regional ERP vendors expanding into logistics

**Number of Targets:** 50-100 potential buyers

### Expected Valuation

**Range:** $2,000,000 - $4,000,000

**Most Likely:** $2,800,000 - $3,500,000

**Deal Structure:**

- Cash at closing: 80-100%
- Limited earnout: 0-20%
- No employment requirements
- Clean exit with minimal ongoing obligations

### Implementation Steps

**Month 1: Preparation**

1. Engage M&A advisor (contingent fee: 5-8% of transaction)
2. Prepare comprehensive data room
3. Create executive summary and pitch deck
4. Gather all technical documentation
5. Document development costs and timeline
6. Prepare demonstration environment
7. Create buyer FAQ document

**Month 2: Positioning**

1. Develop target buyer list with prioritization
2. Research each buyer's strategic needs
3. Customize pitch for each buyer category
4. Prepare financial projections and models
5. Create competitive analysis materials
6. Document unique value propositions
7. Establish confidentiality protocols

**Month 3-4: Outreach**

1. Initial contact with top 20 targets
2. Execute NDA with interested parties
3. Distribute executive summary
4. Schedule initial discovery calls
5. Provide system demonstrations
6. Answer technical questions
7. Qualify serious buyers

**Month 5-6: Due Diligence**

1. Open data room to qualified buyers
2. Provide technical deep dives
3. Answer detailed questions
4. Allow code inspection
5. Demonstrate system capabilities
6. Provide reference materials
7. Address buyer concerns

**Month 7-8: Negotiation**

1. Receive initial offers
2. Negotiate terms with top candidates
3. Select preferred buyer
4. Execute letter of intent
5. Conduct final due diligence
6. Negotiate definitive agreements
7. Prepare for closing

**Month 9-12: Closing**

1. Complete final due diligence
2. Resolve any outstanding issues
3. Execute purchase agreement
4. Transfer all assets and IP
5. Receive payment
6. Provide transition support
7. Complete transaction

### Probability of Success

**Overall Success Rate:** 60-70%

**Risk Factors:**

- No customer validation may deter some buyers
- Price expectations may not align with offers
- Due diligence may reveal unexpected issues
- Market conditions may change

**Mitigation Strategies:**

- Cast wide net with many potential buyers
- Be flexible on terms while maintaining minimum price
- Prepare thoroughly for due diligence
- Consider creative deal structures

### Advantages

- Fastest path to liquidity
- No ongoing operational burden
- Clean exit with minimal contingencies
- Immediate return on investment
- Low execution risk

### Disadvantages

- Lowest potential valuation
- Limited leverage without revenue
- May leave significant value on table
- Quick sale discount typically 20-30%

### Recommended For

- Founders wanting immediate exit
- Those unable to invest more time
- Risk-averse sellers
- Situations requiring quick liquidity

## Exit Option 2: Strategic Sale with Traction

### Strategy Summary

Invest 6-12 months building initial customer traction and proving market demand before initiating formal sale process. Significantly higher valuation potential.

### Timeline

**Total Duration:** 12-18 months

**Phase 1: Traction Building (6 months)**

- Month 1-2: Pilot customer acquisition
- Month 3-4: Implementation and optimization
- Month 5-6: Results documentation and case studies

**Phase 2: Sale Process (6-12 months)**

- Follow Quick Asset Sale process with enhanced positioning

### Target Buyers

**Primary Targets:**

- Strategic WMS competitors
- Enterprise software companies
- Well-funded private equity firms
- SaaS consolidators

**Quality Over Quantity:** 30-50 highly qualified buyers

### Expected Valuation

**Range:** $5,000,000 - $10,000,000

**Most Likely:** $6,000,000 - $8,000,000

**Deal Structure:**

- Cash at closing: 60-70%
- Performance earnout: 30-40%
- Possible consulting period: 3-6 months
- Limited employment requirements

### Traction Building Plan

**Phase 1A: Pilot Customer Acquisition (Months 1-2)**

Target Profile:

- Mid-sized warehouses (50,000 - 200,000 sq ft)
- 20-100 employees
- Current pain points with existing WMS
- Willing to be early adopters
- Reference-able in target industries

Acquisition Strategy:

1. Identify 20-30 potential pilot customers
2. Offer highly attractive pilot terms:
   - Free for 6 months OR
   - $500-1,000/month (cover costs only)
3. Emphasize voice operations benefits
4. Promise white-glove implementation
5. Guarantee dedicated support
6. Request testimonial and case study rights

Target: Secure 2-3 pilot customers

**Phase 1B: Implementation (Months 3-4)**

Implementation Approach:

1. Dedicated onboarding for each pilot
2. Customize for specific workflows
3. Train all users comprehensively
4. Monitor usage and performance daily
5. Optimize based on real-world feedback
6. Document all interactions and improvements
7. Build strong customer relationships

Success Metrics:

- System uptime: 99%+
- User adoption: 80%+
- Productivity improvement: 20%+ documented
- Customer satisfaction: 8/10 or higher
- Zero critical issues

**Phase 1C: Results Documentation (Months 5-6)**

Documentation Requirements:

1. Detailed case studies for each customer
2. Quantified productivity improvements
3. ROI calculations with customer validation
4. Video testimonials from users
5. Before/after workflow comparisons
6. Cost savings analysis
7. Customer satisfaction surveys

Deliverables:

- 3 professional case studies
- 5-10 video testimonials
- ROI calculator with real data
- Performance benchmark report
- Customer reference program

### Enhanced Sale Process

**Advantages Over Quick Sale:**

- Proven product-market fit
- Real-world performance data
- Customer testimonials and references
- Demonstrated demand
- Reduced buyer risk perception

**Valuation Impact:**

- Base increase: +50% to +100%
- Customer traction adds: $1,500,000 - $3,000,000
- Case studies add: $500,000 - $1,000,000
- Proven ROI adds: $500,000 - $1,000,000

**Enhanced Negotiating Position:**

- Multiple competitive buyers likely
- Higher cash percentage at closing
- Better earnout terms
- More favorable deal structure
- Stronger walk-away position

### Implementation Steps

**Months 1-2: Customer Acquisition**

1. Create pilot program offering
2. Build target customer list
3. Develop outreach materials
4. Execute targeted marketing campaign
5. Conduct discovery calls
6. Present pilot program
7. Sign 2-3 pilot agreements

**Months 3-4: Deployment**

1. Onboard pilot customers
2. Deploy systems
3. Train users
4. Monitor performance
5. Optimize workflows
6. Provide exceptional support
7. Build customer relationships

**Months 5-6: Documentation**

1. Measure and document results
2. Create case studies
3. Record testimonials
4. Calculate ROI metrics
5. Prepare reference program
6. Build enhanced pitch materials
7. Quantify value creation

**Months 7-18: Sale Process**

1. Execute Option 1 process with enhanced materials
2. Lead with customer success stories
3. Demonstrate proven demand
4. Leverage multiple interested buyers
5. Negotiate from position of strength
6. Achieve optimal deal terms
7. Close transaction

### Investment Required

**Time Investment:**

- Founder/key person: 20-30 hours/week for 6 months
- Then reduced to sale process management

**Financial Investment:**

- Minimal: Most costs absorbed in pilot pricing
- Marketing and outreach: $5,000 - $10,000
- Sales tools and materials: $3,000 - $5,000
- Legal for pilot agreements: $5,000 - $8,000
- Total: $15,000 - $25,000

**Risk:**

- 6 months of additional time
- Modest financial investment
- Potential pilot customers may not succeed
- Market conditions could change

### Probability of Success

**Overall Success Rate:** 75-85%

**Success Factors:**

- Proven customer demand significantly reduces buyer risk
- Real ROI data removes speculation
- Testimonials build credibility
- Multiple data points create confidence

### Advantages

- Significantly higher valuation
- Stronger negotiating position
- Multiple competitive buyers likely
- Better deal terms
- Proven market validation
- Reduced buyer risk perception

### Disadvantages

- Requires 6 months additional time
- Modest financial investment needed
- Must manage customer relationships
- Pilot customers could fail
- Market timing risk

### Recommended For

- Founders with 6-12 months available
- Those seeking optimal valuation
- Comfortable with pilot customer management
- Willing to invest modestly for higher return
- Confident in product capabilities

## Exit Option 3: Build Revenue Business

### Strategy Summary

Operate as revenue-generating business for 18-24 months, prove scalable business model, then sell at revenue multiple or raise institutional capital.

### Timeline

**Total Duration:** 24-36 months

**Phase 1: Foundation (Months 1-6)**

- Raise seed capital or bootstrap
- Build sales and marketing foundation
- Hire initial team
- Refine product based on early customers

**Phase 2: Growth (Months 7-18)**

- Scale customer acquisition
- Prove repeatable sales model
- Build customer success function
- Expand team strategically

**Phase 3: Exit (Months 19-24)**

- Achieve $1M+ ARR milestone
- Initiate sale process OR raise Series A
- Demonstrate scalability
- Execute transaction

### Target Outcome

**Exit via Acquisition:**

- Valuation: $15,000,000 - $30,000,000
- Multiple: 10-15x ARR
- Buyer: Strategic acquirer or growth equity firm

**Exit via Funding:**

- Series A raise: $5,000,000 - $15,000,000
- Valuation: $20,000,000 - $40,000,000
- Eventual exit: $50,000,000+

### Financial Requirements

**Seed Capital Needed:** $1,000,000 - $2,000,000

**Use of Funds:**

- Sales team: $400,000 - $600,000 (2-3 sales reps + VP Sales)
- Marketing: $200,000 - $300,000
- Product development: $200,000 - $300,000
- Customer success: $100,000 - $150,000
- Operations and admin: $100,000 - $150,000
- Working capital: $100,000 - $200,000

**Revenue Targets:**

- Year 1: $500,000 - $750,000 ARR
- Year 2: $1,500,000 - $2,500,000 ARR
- Year 3: $4,000,000 - $6,000,000 ARR

### Probability of Success

**Overall Success Rate:** 30-40%

**Success Factors:**

- Much higher execution risk
- Requires significant capital
- Must build entire business infrastructure
- Sales cycles are long (6-12 months)
- Market competition is intense

### Advantages

- Highest potential return
- Build sustainable business
- Multiple exit optionality
- Prove scalability
- Generate cash flow

### Disadvantages

- Highest risk profile
- Longest time horizon
- Significant capital required
- Must build full team
- Operational complexity
- May still fail after years of effort

### Recommended For

- Founders committed to building company
- Those with access to capital
- Experienced in scaling SaaS businesses
- Long-term vision (3-5 years)
- Comfortable with high risk/high reward

## Decision Framework

### Key Questions to Determine Optimal Path

**Time Availability:**

- Available 6 months part-time: Option 1
- Available 12 months part-time: Option 2
- Available 24+ months full-time: Option 3

**Financial Position:**

- Need liquidity soon: Option 1
- Can invest $25K and wait 12-18 months: Option 2
- Can invest/raise $1M+ and wait 24+ months: Option 3

**Risk Tolerance:**

- Low risk preference: Option 1
- Moderate risk for higher return: Option 2
- High risk for maximum return: Option 3

**Personal Goals:**

- Ready to move on: Option 1
- Prove concept then exit: Option 2
- Build lasting company: Option 3

### Recommended Approach

**For Most Founders: Option 2 (Strategic Sale with Traction)**

**Rationale:**

- Best risk-adjusted return
- Manageable time investment
- Significant valuation improvement
- Modest capital requirement
- High probability of success
- Proves value without full business build

**Expected Outcome:**

- Sale price: $5,000,000 - $8,000,000
- Timeline: 12-18 months
- Time commitment: Part-time for 6 months, then sale process
- Investment: $15,000 - $25,000
- Probability: 75-85%

## Conclusion

Logivox has three viable exit paths, each appropriate for different circumstances. Option 2 (Strategic Sale with Traction) offers the best balance of risk, return, time investment, and probability of success for most situations.

The key is to make a clear decision, commit to the chosen path, and execute systematically. All three options can result in successful exits with proper planning and execution.
